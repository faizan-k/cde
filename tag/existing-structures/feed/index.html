Many defects within a structure are not visible through conventional inspection methods. Moisture intrusion, insulation failure, and energy loss often remain hidden until they cause significant damage.

Thermographic inspection provides a non-destructive way to detect such hidden issues.

What is Thermographic Inspection

Thermographic inspection uses infrared imaging to detect temperature variations within a structure. These variations indicate underlying issues such as moisture, voids, or insulation defects.

Our Approach

1. Infrared Survey

The structure is scanned using thermal imaging equipment to detect temperature differences.

2. Data Interpretation

Thermal images are analyzed to identify anomalies and potential defects.

3. Defect Identification

Areas affected by moisture intrusion, insulation failure, or energy loss are identified.

4. Reporting

Findings are documented with images and technical explanations.

Scope of Services

  • Infrared thermal imaging surveys
  • Detection of moisture and leakage
  • Identification of insulation defects
  • Analysis of thermal performance

Key Outcomes

  • Early detection of hidden defects
  • Improved energy efficiency
  • Reduced maintenance costs
  • Non-destructive inspection

Applications

  • Building envelopes
  • Roof systems
  • Electrical and mechanical systems
  • Energy efficiency assessments

Engineering Value

Thermographic inspection provides valuable insights without damaging the structure, enabling timely intervention and improved performance.

Corrosion of reinforcement is one of the primary causes of deterioration in concrete structures. If not properly identified and addressed, it can lead to cracking, spalling, and reduction in structural capacity.

A detailed corrosion investigation is essential to understand the extent and cause of damage.

What is Corrosion Investigation

Corrosion investigation identifies the presence, extent, and mechanisms of corrosion affecting reinforcement within a structure.

Our Approach

1. Visual Assessment

Initial inspection identifies visible signs such as rust staining, cracking, and spalling.

2. Testing and Measurement

Electrochemical and material testing methods are used to assess corrosion activity and extent.

3. Chloride and Carbonation Analysis

Testing is conducted to determine chloride content and carbonation depth, which are key factors in corrosion.

4. Mapping and Analysis

Corrosion-prone areas are mapped and analyzed to understand patterns and severity.

5. Root Cause Identification

Underlying causes such as environmental exposure, poor construction practices, or material deficiencies are identified.

Scope of Services

  • Corrosion mapping
  • Chloride content testing
  • Half-cell potential measurements
  • Carbonation depth analysis
  • Root cause evaluation

Key Outcomes

  • Identification of corrosion mechanisms
  • Accurate assessment of damage extent
  • Targeted repair and protection strategies
  • Prevention of further deterioration

Applications

  • Reinforced concrete structures
