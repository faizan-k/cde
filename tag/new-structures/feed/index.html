Cracks are one of the most common indicators of structural issues. However, not all cracks are harmful. Proper assessment is required to determine their cause, severity, and impact on performance.

Understanding cracks is essential for ensuring structural safety and durability.

What is Crack Assessment

Crack assessment involves analyzing cracks to determine their type, cause, and effect on the structure.

Our Approach

1. Crack Identification

Cracks are identified and classified based on type, location, and pattern.

2. Measurement and Monitoring

Crack width and progression are measured to assess severity.

3. Cause Analysis

Factors such as loading, shrinkage, thermal effects, and corrosion are analyzed.

4. Risk Evaluation

The impact of cracks on structural performance is evaluated.

5. Recommendation

Appropriate repair or monitoring strategies are provided.

Scope of Services

  • Crack mapping and documentation
  • Measurement and monitoring
  • Cause analysis
  • Risk assessment
  • Repair recommendations

Key Outcomes

  • Accurate understanding of crack behavior
  • Identification of structural risks
  • Appropriate corrective actions
  • Prevention of further deterioration

Applications

  • Concrete structures
  • Buildings and infrastructure
  • Industrial facilities
  • Structures with visible cracking

Engineering Value

Proper crack assessment ensures that critical issues are addressed while avoiding unnecessary repairs for non-structural cracks.

Temperature variations within concrete structures can lead to thermal stresses, which may result in cracking and long-term durability issues. This is particularly critical in large structural elements and extreme environmental conditions.

Thermal modeling helps predict and manage these effects during design and construction.

What is Thermal Modeling

Thermal modeling is the process of simulating temperature distribution within a structure to understand its impact on structural behavior and durability.

Our Approach

1. Data Collection

Material properties, environmental conditions, and construction sequences are analyzed.

2. Simulation

Advanced modeling tools are used to simulate temperature variations over time.

3. Stress Analysis

Thermal stresses and potential cracking risks are evaluated.

4. Design Optimization

Design modifications are recommended to minimize thermal effects.

Scope of Services

  • Thermal analysis of structures
  • Simulation of temperature variations
  • Crack risk evaluation
  • Recommendations for design improvement

Key Outcomes

  • Reduced risk of thermal cracking
  • Improved structural durability
  • Optimized design for environmental conditions

Applications

  • Mass concrete structures
  • Foundations and slabs
  • Infrastructure projects
  • High-temperature environments

Engineering Value

Thermal modeling enables proactive design decisions, reducing the risk of early-age and long-term cracking.

Defects introduced during construction can significantly affect the long-term performance of a structure. Issues such as improper curing, honeycombing, and early-age cracking can compromise durability if not identified and addressed early.

Concrete evaluation during construction helps detect and correct such issues before they escalate.

What is Concrete Evaluation

Concrete evaluation is the process of assessing the quality and integrity of concrete during or shortly after construction to identify defects and ensure compliance with design requirements.

Our Approach

1. Visual Inspection

Concrete surfaces are inspected to identify visible defects such as cracks, voids, and surface irregularities.

2. Quality Assessment

Workmanship and construction practices are evaluated to identify potential causes of defects.

3. Testing and Verification

Testing methods are used to assess strength, uniformity, and integrity of concrete.

4. Defect Analysis

Identified defects are analyzed to determine their cause and potential impact.

5. Corrective Recommendations

Appropriate measures are recommended to address defects and prevent future issues.

Scope of Services

  • Identification of construction defects
  • Assessment of concrete quality
  • Testing and evaluation
  • Root cause analysis
  • Recommendations for corrective actions

Key Outcomes

  • Early detection of defects
  • Improved construction quality
  • Prevention of long-term deterioration
  • Enhanced structural performance

Applications

  • Newly constructed buildings
  • Infrastructure projects
  • Concrete structures under construction
  • Quality control programs

Durability is a fundamental requirement for any structure expected to perform over its intended service life. Many structural issues arise not because of poor construction, but due to lack of proper durability considerations during the design stage.

Durability planning ensures that structures are designed to resist environmental exposure, material degradation, and long-term deterioration from the very beginning.

What is Durability Planning

Durability planning is the process of integrating durability requirements into the design and construction stages of a project. It focuses on ensuring that the structure can achieve its intended service life with minimal maintenance.

Our Approach

1. Exposure Assessment

Environmental conditions such as coastal exposure, industrial pollution, humidity, and temperature variations are evaluated to understand potential risks.

2. Material Selection

Materials are selected based on performance requirements and resistance to environmental conditions. This includes evaluating concrete quality, reinforcement protection, and protective systems.

3. Design Integration

Durability requirements are incorporated into structural design, ensuring that detailing, cover, and construction practices support long-term performance.

4. Performance Criteria Definition

Clear performance criteria are established to guide construction and quality control.

Scope of Services

  • Environmental exposure analysis
  • Material selection guidance
  • Durability design criteria
  • Integration with structural design
  • Recommendations for construction practices

Key Outcomes

  • Structures designed for long-term performance
  • Reduced maintenance requirements
  • Improved resistance to deterioration
  • Enhanced lifecycle efficiency

Applications

  • Coastal and marine structures
  • Infrastructure projects
  • Industrial facilities
  • High-exposure environments

Engineering Value

Durability planning ensures that structures are built not just to perform initially, but to endure over time with minimal intervention.
