Cracks are one of the most common indicators of structural issues. However, not all cracks are harmful. Proper assessment is required to determine their cause, severity, and impact on performance.

Understanding cracks is essential for ensuring structural safety and durability.

What is Crack Assessment

Crack assessment involves analyzing cracks to determine their type, cause, and effect on the structure.

Our Approach

1. Crack Identification

Cracks are identified and classified based on type, location, and pattern.

2. Measurement and Monitoring

Crack width and progression are measured to assess severity.

3. Cause Analysis

Factors such as loading, shrinkage, thermal effects, and corrosion are analyzed.

4. Risk Evaluation

The impact of cracks on structural performance is evaluated.

5. Recommendation

Appropriate repair or monitoring strategies are provided.

Scope of Services

  • Crack mapping and documentation
  • Measurement and monitoring
  • Cause analysis
  • Risk assessment
  • Repair recommendations

Key Outcomes

  • Accurate understanding of crack behavior
  • Identification of structural risks
  • Appropriate corrective actions
  • Prevention of further deterioration

Applications

  • Concrete structures
  • Buildings and infrastructure
  • Industrial facilities
  • Structures with visible cracking

Engineering Value

Proper crack assessment ensures that critical issues are addressed while avoiding unnecessary repairs for non-structural cracks.

The structure is scanned using thermal imaging equipment to detect temperature differences.

2. Data Interpretation

Thermal images are analyzed to identify anomalies and potential defects.

3. Defect Identification

Areas affected by moisture intrusion, insulation failure, or energy loss are identified.

4. Reporting

Findings are documented with images and technical explanations.

Corrosion investigation identifies the presence, extent, and mechanisms of corrosion affecting reinforcement within a structure.

Initial inspection identifies visible signs such as rust staining, cracking, and spalling.

2. Testing and Measurement

Electrochemical and material testing methods are used to assess corrosion activity and extent.

3. Chloride and Carbonation Analysis

Testing is conducted to determine chloride content and carbonation depth, which are key factors in corrosion.

4. Mapping and Analysis

Corrosion-prone areas are mapped and analyzed to understand patterns and severity.

5. Root Cause Identification

Underlying causes such as environmental exposure, poor construction practices, or material deficiencies are identified.

Scope of Services

  • Root cause evaluation
